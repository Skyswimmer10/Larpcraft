# Human Values proposal: review before implementation

## Five-sentence review

1. The strongest improvement is choosing only the values at stake and showing how they could change, which is clearer than answering every value in the library.
2. The implication chain, different stakeholders and reveal schedule would help explain why the same event matters differently to different people.
3. The main drawback is that charge formulas, suspense scores and option totals could create clutter and suggest more certainty about emotion than the designer actually has.
4. Some rules are too rigid: a scene can be meaningful without a loss, a difficult choice need not have equal numerical totals, and being a follower is not automatically worse than being a leader.
5. Larpcraft can reuse the saving, context and controls developed for FATE and Kolb while making Human Values focus on stakes and choices instead of another long questionnaire.

## Recommended design

- Simple: select one to three values, describe their starting state and possible changes, explain why they matter to someone, then describe the event or choice.
- Advanced: add different stakeholder perspectives, alternative outcomes, implications, information timing and changes across scenes or acts.
- Make dilemmas optional; allow celebration, relief, protection and recovery as well as sacrifice and conflict.
- Treat importance and expected intensity as the writer's ratings, not measured emotions; do not add different values into an authoritative best-choice score.
- Let people define the state names and their meaning in this situation. Knowledge, deception, suspicion and certainty need not form a single equally spaced scale.
- Separate what is true in the world from what each person believes or has learned about it. A reveal may change someone's understanding while the underlying event happened earlier.
- Keep imports and suggestions explicit. A Kolb failure does not prove a character is unskilled; an old positive/negative preference does not establish the ending of a story.

## Additional suggestion: “What matters to whom—and why now?”

Give every selected value one short human explanation. For example: “To Mara, the key is the last promise her sister kept; losing it would mean abandoning that bond.” This connects the abstract value to the specific story and can become part of the final editable paragraph.

Show threatened and protected values as well as actual changes: safe → threatened → safe can produce a rescue and relief even though the first and last states match. Include a place to record the player's response after a playtest, separately from the intended feeling.

These are design recommendations, not additional rules attributed to the book. Sylvester describes the book's concepts as tools that leave authorship with the designer: https://tynansylvester.com/book/

This review preceded implementation. After approval, Simple and Advanced modes were implemented; see `human-values-wizard.md` and `human-values-wizard-verification.md`.
